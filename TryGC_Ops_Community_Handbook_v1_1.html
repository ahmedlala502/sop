How to use this

Find your role in Part 1 or Part 2 and read the card. It is the whole job description — there is no longer one anywhere else. Then read the core curriculum in Part 3 and your function’s curriculum beneath it. Part 4 answers the things people ask in the corridor rather than in the meeting. Part 5 is the certification test; the answer keys are published deliberately, because the point is that everyone can pass, not that some people fail.

One rule that applies to every card in here

Every role owns one number, and it is a number that role can actually influence. If yours is not, it is the wrong number — raise it at your first monthly scorecard and it gets changed. Supporting measures exist, but only one number decides whether the function is working.

4.1 The change itself

Twelve people get named as leads. What happens to the other thirty-two?

You get a one-to-one within five working days of the announcement — what was strong, what to build, and the next concrete opportunity. That is action R43 with a deadline, not an intention. The route is published on the same day as the names: certification, then the capability matrix, then Flex or cover, then the first lead vacancy. And separately from any of that, by 3 September every one of the 45 has one number they own. The leads are not the only people who get clarity out of this.

Are the leads already decided?

No. The criteria are published before any name is discussed, self-nomination is open to everyone for three days, and anyone can nominate themselves or a colleague for any function — not only their own. The decision is made by the Head of Regional Operations with the Quality Lead checking that the criteria were applied consistently. If self-nomination shows somebody is already leading a function in practice, that appointment becomes a recognition rather than a promotion, which is faster and lands better.

Does becoming a lead mean more money?

That is decision D2 and it is due on 20 August, announced with the names. Any defensible answer is acceptable — including 'title now, reviewed at 90 days against the scorecard'. What is not acceptable is silence, and that is why it is on the decision list rather than left to drift.

I am being made a lead but my current caseload is not going anywhere. How is that going to work?

No appointment is announced until your current work is written down next to somebody else's name. That is action R44, due before the announcement. Your split is published too: roughly half leading and half executing in month one, moving to mostly leading by month three, reviewed monthly. If you are still carrying a full execution load at 60 days, that is a resourcing failure we made — raise it and it gets fixed.

Is anybody losing their job?

No. Roster headcount goes from 44 to 45, but nobody new joins. The thirteen Operations Specialists all move into named functions, and the Kuwait seat is filled by converting the person who already works that market full-time from outside the roster. External hires: zero. Nobody is moving branch either — Cairo's four stay in Cairo.

The Operations Specialist pool was useful. Why break it up?

It was useful, and that is stated in the review: it gave cover and absorbed surges, and it is being used badly rather than chosen badly. What it cost is that nobody in it owned a number, and the most urgent function in the department — Follow-Up, which carries the 24-hour fix SLA — was staffed by whoever happened to be free. The cover is kept, in two forms: a Flex pair with a lead and a purpose, and cross-skilling that now applies to everyone rather than only to Flex.

4.2 Measurement and SLAs

The task times do not match reality. Am I going to be marked down for that?

No. Task durations are expected averages, and the agreed position — which came from the Community team, not from management — is that duration is an improvement indicator first. When a task repeatedly overruns, the question is whether there is an unnecessary manual step, an inefficient workflow, a training gap, a system fix available, or whether the SLA itself is simply wrong. Action R53 re-examines every Task Manager SLA against observed durations by 3 September.

What if my whole team misses the same measure?

Then it is treated as a process failure and the process gets corrected before anybody has a performance conversation about it. That rule is written into section 3.4 of the review specifically so that a badly-set target cannot become an individual's problem.

Why do I only get one number?

Because a person with six numbers has none. The single number is the thing your function actually controls — you should be able to influence it directly. If you cannot, it is the wrong number and it gets changed; say so at your first monthly scorecard.

The escalation windows look tight. What if a 4-hour checkpoint is unrealistic?

Escalation volume is itself a KPI for the first 30 days. If a checkpoint escalates on more than 20% of occasions, the window is wrong — not the person — and it is re-set at the 30-day review before it touches anyone's scorecard. This is deliberate: nobody knows yet whether these windows are realistic, and a window that is wrong teaches people to ignore escalations, which is worse than not having them.

How is my monthly scorecard weighted?

Results 40%, process compliance 20%, quality outcomes 15%, responsiveness 10%, judgement and initiative 15%. The majority comes from system data rather than impression, which is the point. Bands: exceeds ≥90, meets 75–89, needs improvement 60–74 with a structured 30-day plan reviewed weekly, unsatisfactory below 60.

4.3 The system and the record

Why does everything have to go into the Task Manager during the shift?

Because a record updated at the end of the day cannot be used during the day. Live logging means your manager can see your actual workload before assigning more, priorities are visible, completed work is traceable, campaign history stays intact, and SLA figures are real rather than reconstructed. This came from the Community team's own review — they identified it as the thing creating the problem.

Can I just send a WhatsApp message instead of raising a task?

You can send the message — messages are for alerts. But any request that creates work carries a task ID. That is the rule from action R10. A request without an ID cannot be aged, counted, prioritised or handed over, and it is invisible the moment you go on leave.

There are two sheets and they never match. Which one is right?

Neither, today — that is exactly the problem, and it is gap G1. From mid-September the campaign record is the only source and the client sheet becomes a generated view you can read but not type into. Until then, the shared control board is the single place to look and any discrepancy goes to Quality the same day.

What happens to WhatsApp?

It stays as a channel, and creators keep being reachable there. What changes is that replies get logged against the creator record at the moment they arrive rather than living in a chat thread, so nobody has to reconstruct a dispute by scrolling. The team is being renamed from Whatsapp to Outreach for the same reason: a team named after a tool is a team where the tool is running the process.

4.4 Clients

A client asked for something in a meeting. What do I actually do with it?

It becomes a line on the meeting record with a request, a priority, an owner, a due date and a status. Not a note in the minutes — a line with a name and a date against it. This is action R50 and it applies from 27 August, manually on the shared board until the module ships. The reason it is critical rather than merely useful: a promise nobody tracks is lost silently, the client remembers it, and no escalation is ever raised.

A client we have not worked with in a year has come back. Where do I start?

The Client 360° profile: previous campaigns, results, issues, feedback, meeting history, decisions, complaints and quality expectations, and what was promised and whether it was done. Then look at their recent activity for new branches, products or offers, and contact them with context rather than a generic approach. If the profile does not exist yet for that client, building it is the first step — R51 stands these up from 15 September.

Who talks to the client?

The Client & Campaign Specialist for that market, so the client hears one voice. The Campaign Owner owns what the numbers mean and the recommendation. Anything that changes scope, price or a deadline escalates to the Community Lead before it is said to the client.

The client has not approved the list and it has been two days. Do I keep chasing?

At 48 hours the approval conversation triggers — HC5. That is a conversation, not another reminder: a stalled approval is a relationship signal, not a data problem. Find out why they are hesitating, and whether the brief or the criteria need to change.

4.5 Working in the new structure

Do I specialise now, or do I stay general?

Both, deliberately. You own one primary area — that is your specialty and your number. You are also certified in at least one neighbouring function: the one you hand work to or take it from, because that is where handover defects happen. This came from the Community team's own conclusion that total specialisation creates dependency risk.

What is the Flex pair actually for?

Surge and absence cover, deployed each day against whichever function has the oldest breached queue, and recorded against that function. Flex is deliberately small because cross-skilling now covers most absences. If two or more functions breach in the same week during the first 60 days, one Follow-Up seat moves temporarily to Flex and the breach is logged as evidence for the capacity review.

How do I hand work to another team?

By changing the stage status, not by sending a message. If the receiving team cannot see it in the system, it has not been handed over and you still own it. Each of the fourteen handovers has an SLA and a required piece of evidence — H1 to H14 are published from 3 September.

Something is stuck and it is not my fault. What do I do?

Write it in four fields: what happened, what you have already tried, what you need, and what it costs if we wait. Send it to the level that owns the decision — L1 function lead same day, L2 Operations Manager 4 hours, L3 Head 24 hours, L4 CEO 48 hours. An escalation missing any of the four fields comes back to you rather than getting actioned, so include all four. And to say it plainly: raising a problem is not complaining, and the first few escalations will be answered fast and visibly so that people believe that.

When will I be trained?

Everyone is trained on the 14-stage flow and the checkpoint windows by 15 September (R29). Function training runs alongside it, delivered by your own lead. Certification is by 15 October (R30), and certification gates account ownership — which is the point of doing it.
